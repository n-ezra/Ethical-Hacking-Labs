## 🔬 Scan Methodology

FIN Scan works by sending TCP packets with only the **FIN flag** set.

### Process

1. Nmap sends a TCP FIN packet to the target port.
2. According to RFC 793:
   - Closed ports respond with **RST** packets.
   - Open ports ignore the FIN packet and send no response.
3. If no response is received, Nmap labels the port as **open|filtered**.
4. If an ICMP filtering message is received, the port is considered filtered.

### Purpose

FIN scans are commonly used for:

- Firewall evasion
- Stealth reconnaissance
- Port state analysis
- Security assessments

## 🔐 Meaning of "open|filtered"

A port is marked as **open|filtered** when:

- No response is received.
- Nmap cannot determine whether:
  - The port is open, or
  - A firewall filtered the probe.

### Common Causes

- Stateful firewalls
- Packet filtering routers
- Silent packet drops
- Security appliances
- Open ports ignoring FIN packets

## ⚖️ Advantages of FIN Scan

- Stealthier than SYN scans
- May bypass simple packet filters
- Useful for firewall testing
- Generates less obvious scanning activity

---

## ❌ Limitations

- Many modern firewalls detect and block FIN scans
- Results often appear as open|filtered
- Less reliable than SYN scans
- Not effective against some operating systems (especially Windows)
